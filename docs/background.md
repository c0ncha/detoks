# Background

A summary of our CT3 deliverables: the presentation, Argument Maps 1 and 2, and the Alignment Note. This is the ethical case the app is built to demonstrate.

## Harms we target

1. **Information echo chambers**: closed loops that keep users inside one set of viewpoints by reinforcing what they already believe and filtering out disagreement.
2. **Lack of real-world connection**: passive scrolling replaces in-person connection, leaving users more isolated despite heavy platform use.
3. **Addictive variable reward loops**: unpredictable rewards built to maximize continuous scrolling and session length at the expense of user agency.

## Alignment strategy

1. **Real-time content classification layer**: every uploaded video is classified for potential harms (e.g., negative body image, dangerous activities).
2. **User-centric safety caps**: a changed recommender that limits how much potentially harmful content users see, with daily time limits and more video diversity.
3. **Social features**: collaborative content creation, plus scheduling with family and friends.

## Scenarios

- **Bob**: hours of nightly scrolling pull him into an echo chamber (manosphere content) and away from his friends. *Resolution:* content diversity breaks the echo chamber, and time limits restore real-world time.
- **Katie**: a single parent whose kids spend hours on TikTok when relatives babysit, and who struggle to focus. *Resolution:* parent content controls, and a push toward real-world challenges and experiments the kids do together.
- **George**: shares challenge videos with his friend group, who then do them in person. *Resolution:* the app supports this directly by helping friends find, plan and record challenges together.

## Argument Map 1: should platforms put user well-being ahead of watch time?

**Agree**
- Prioritize well-being to reduce mental-health harms. A one-week social media break improved well-being [Lambert et al., 2022]. Passive Facebook browsing reduced affective well-being [Verduyn et al., 2015]. TikTok's algorithm heavily weights watch time, so it treats long passive viewing as a positive signal.
- Prioritize meaningful social interaction. Personalized communication from close friends is linked to better well-being [Burke & Kraut, 2016]. Recommenders can weight comments and shares alongside watch time.

**Disagree**
- Competitiveness: TikTok predicts preferences from watch duration, so weakening that signal weakens personalization. In a restriction experiment, users switched to instant messaging instead [Collis & Eggers, 2022]. TikTok has already drawn engagement away from Meta [Meta 2025 10-K].
- Financial sustainability: ads drive revenue; Meta's ad revenue grew alongside a 12% rise in ad impressions [Meta 2025 10-K]. Shorter sessions mean fewer impressions, while infrastructure costs stay the same.

## Argument Map 2: must platforms maximize watch time to be an effective product?

- **Agree**: competitors are easy to reach, and users will switch; the whole ecosystem has moved to short-form video (Reels, Shorts); most users still choose current platforms.
- **Disagree**: some users avoid social media on purpose, and a product built around different goals can appeal to them. Users can make an informed choice. Effectiveness isn't only short-term financial: a viable prototype can inform policy, and giving up some short-term competitiveness is an investment in a healthier ecosystem.

## Our responses (Alignment Note)

- Maximizing watch time isn't necessary for profit. Ads remain viable, and engaged, well-targeted audiences may be worth more per impression than mindless scrollers.
- The target is not to beat TikTok. It is a **proof of concept** that a well-being-first platform is workable, which makes regulation that limits pure watch-time optimization feasible.
- Users who return to harmful platforms often do so because of network effects and data that can't move with them, not by autonomous choice. Cutting out social media entirely tends to fail ([Collis & Eggers]), so users need a healthier middle ground.
- Quality over quantity: "the best 100 videos a day," highlight recaps, and prompts to share with friends create a positive feedback loop of return visits and word of mouth.
- A large group of people avoid current apps because of the harm. They are an underserved market.
